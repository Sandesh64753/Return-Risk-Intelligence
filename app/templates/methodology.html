Technical Documentation

Model Methodology & Validation

Comprehensive overview of dataset formulation, pre-shipment leakage auditing, model selection, and operational threshold calibration.

1. Problem Formulation & Operational Timing

The objective of this machine learning system is to classify whether a purchased product will be returned (returned = 1) or retained (returned = 0).

Crucial Constraint • Pre-Shipment Prediction Point

Predictions are evaluated at the moment an order is approved, prior to dispatch and delivery. Any data that only manifests after fulfillment (delivery delays, customer support calls, chat interactions) represents future information and must be strictly excluded to prevent target leakage.

2. Pre-Shipment Leakage Audit

During exploratory data analysis, all dataset columns underwent a rigorous audit against their temporal availability at order placement:

Candidate Feature Audit Decision Technical Rationale
delayed_delivery Excluded Post-purchase event; only known after courier transit has commenced or failed.
customer_support_calls Excluded Support interactions occur overwhelmingly post-purchase regarding tracking or returns.
chat_interactions Excluded Customer support chat logs represent post-purchase inquiries.
total_support_contacts Excluded Exact linear dependency (calls + chats); creates rank deficiency and leakage.
*_return_rate (Product / Brand / Seller) Excluded Historical rate aggregates risk leaking current/future order labels unless verified point-in-time safe.
product_category, sub_category, brand Retained Catalog specifications confirmed available at the moment of listing.
product_price, discount_percent Retained Transaction checkout financial values finalized before warehouse routing.
seller_rating, defect_rate, fulfillment_type Retained Established merchant reliability metrics and dispatch routing choice.
shipping_distance_km, quantity, payment_method Retained Logistics coordinates, basket volume, and checkout payment mechanism.

3. Data Cleaning & Feature Transformation

Numeric Pipeline
  • Median Imputation: Handles missing numeric fields (e.g. ratings or review counts) without sensitivity to extreme outliers.
  • StandardScaler: Standardizes all continuous features to zero mean and unit variance for linear model convergence.
  • Price Log Transform: Raw product_price exhibited high positive skewness (> 1.0); transformed via np.log1p(price).
Categorical Pipeline
  • Most-Frequent Imputation: Replaces missing categorical values with training-set modes.
  • One-Hot Encoding: Encodes discrete categories with handle_unknown='ignore' to ensure unseen categories in production do not break inference.
  • Standardization: Whitespace trimmed and string formatting normalized.

4. Class Imbalance & Estimator Selection

The dataset exhibits moderate class imbalance, with returns representing approximately 30.4% of total orders.

Why Balanced Class Weighting (No SMOTE)

Instead of synthetic oversampling (SMOTE), class weighting (class_weight='balanced') was implemented directly within the loss function. SMOTE interpolates artificial points between high-dimensional one-hot encoded categories, generating unrealistic combinations and distorting the calibration of predicted probabilities.

Why Logistic Regression Selected

In validation comparison against Decision Trees and Random Forests, regularized Logistic Regression achieved the highest validation F1 score. Furthermore, linear models produce well-calibrated probabilities, do not overfit noisy customer behavior, and exhibit stable inference latency.

Threshold Tuning Rationale (τ = 0.3974)

In retail logistics operations, a missed return (False Negative) is substantially more costly than a false alert (False Positive) due to two-way freight, handling, restocking, and inventory depreciation. The decision threshold was calibrated on the validation set precision-recall curve to achieve at least 90% recall, resulting in 88.75% test recall in production.

6. Limitations & Responsible Deployment

Statistical Association vs. Causality: Feature coefficients and probability estimates describe statistical correlations within historical logistics data. They do not demonstrate direct customer causation. For example, negative brand coefficients reflect baseline return rate differences conditional on other covariates, not merchant culpability.

Data Drift Monitoring: Customer return behaviors shift across seasonal sale periods (e.g., holiday peak discount surges). Continuous monitoring of feature distributions and periodic threshold recalibration are recommended.

Operational Decision-Support: This model is designed strictly for logistics risk prioritization (packaging reinforcement, proactive customer confirmation) and is not intended to automate punitive customer interventions.
